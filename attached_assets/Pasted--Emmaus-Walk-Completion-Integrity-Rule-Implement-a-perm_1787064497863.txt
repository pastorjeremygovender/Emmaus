# Emmaus Walk Completion Integrity Rule

Implement a permanent completion rule for all Emmaus Walks.

## Core Rule

A Walk must NEVER be marked complete simply because the user completed the final or highest-numbered Step.

A Walk is complete only when every required Step belonging to that Walk has been individually completed by that user.

Example:

A Walk contains:

* Day 1
* Day 2
* Day 3
* Day 4
* Day 5

If the user opens Day 5 and marks it Finished without completing Days 1–4:

The Walk must NOT be marked complete.

Progress should be:

1 of 5 completed

not:

Walk Complete

## Completion calculation

Walk completion must be calculated from actual Step completion records.

Conceptually:

completed required Steps = total required Steps

Only when those values match may Emmaus:

* mark the Walk complete
* show the Walk Complete experience
* record the Walk completion date
* trigger any completion-related progress
* recommend progression based on completion
* display the Walk as completed elsewhere in Emmaus

Do not use any logic equivalent to:

currentStep === finalStep

or:

highestCompletedStep === totalSteps

as proof that the Walk has been completed.

## Non-sequential completion

Do not automatically force sequential locking as part of this change.

If the existing Walk allows a user to open Steps in a different order, preserve that behaviour.

For example:

User completes:

Day 5

Progress = 1 of 5.

Then Day 2.

Progress = 2 of 5.

Then Day 1.

Progress = 3 of 5.

The Walk remains active until all five required Steps are completed.

## Finished button on final Step

If the user presses Finished on Day 5 while earlier required Steps remain incomplete:

1. Mark Day 5 complete.
2. Do NOT mark the Walk complete.
3. Do NOT show the Walk Complete screen yet.
4. Return the user to the Walk overview/progress experience.
5. Clearly show which Steps remain incomplete.

Use gentle Emmaus language rather than an error.

For example:

"You've completed this Step. There are still parts of this Walk waiting for you."

Do not shame the user or tell them they completed the Walk incorrectly.

## Walk Complete availability

The authored Walk Complete experience should become available only when all required Steps have been completed.

Walk Introduction does not count toward the numerical Step total unless the existing architecture explicitly treats it as a required completion record.

Walk Complete itself does not count as one of the numbered required Steps.

For a five-Step Walk:

Required completion = Day 1 + Day 2 + Day 3 + Day 4 + Day 5.

Then:

Walk Complete becomes available.

## Progress calculation

Progress indicators throughout Emmaus must use the same source of truth.

Examples:

0 completed → 0 of 5
Day 5 only → 1 of 5
Days 1, 2 and 5 → 3 of 5
Days 1–5 → 5 of 5 → Walk Complete available

Do not infer progress from the highest Day number reached.

## Existing users

Do not reset legitimate existing user progress.

Before changing historical Walk completion records, inspect how existing completion data is stored.

If some Walks were incorrectly marked complete solely because the final Step was completed, do not blindly erase user history.

Make the new integrity rule authoritative for future completion calculations while preserving legitimate Step completion records.

If historical repair is necessary, report what would change before performing any destructive migration.

## Journeys

Apply the same principle upward.

A Journey must not be considered complete merely because its final Walk was completed.

A Journey is complete only when every required Walk belonging to that Journey has been completed.

Therefore:

Step completion determines Walk completion.

Walk completion determines Journey completion.

This must form one consistent progression model.

## Testing

Test these scenarios on a temporary five-Step Walk.

### Test 1

Complete Day 5 only.

Expected:

* Day 5 = complete
* Walk progress = 1 of 5
* Walk = not complete
* Walk Complete experience = unavailable

### Test 2

Complete Days 1, 3 and 5.

Expected:

* progress = 3 of 5
* Walk = not complete

### Test 3

Complete Days 1–4 but not Day 5.

Expected:

* progress = 4 of 5
* Walk = not complete

### Test 4

Complete all five Steps in any order.

Expected:

* progress = 5 of 5
* Walk = complete
* authored Walk Complete experience becomes available exactly once

### Test 5

Verify that completing Walk Complete itself does not incorrectly add another numbered Step to progress.

## Scope

Do not redesign Walks.

Do not change authored content.

Do not change the current five-Step content we're building in Come and See.

Do not introduce mandatory sequential locking.

This is a progression-integrity fix only.

The permanent Emmaus rule is:

A Step is complete when that Step is completed.

A Walk is complete when all required Steps are completed.

A Journey is complete when all required Walks are completed.
