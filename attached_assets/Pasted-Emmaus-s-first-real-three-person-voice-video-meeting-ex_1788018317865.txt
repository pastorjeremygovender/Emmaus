Emmaus’s first real three-person voice/video meeting exposed serious reliability and usability problems. This is not a cosmetic task.

Do not add unrelated features. Audit the existing implementation, determine the root cause of every issue below, implement the fixes, and test with at least three simultaneous independent clients.

Preserve all working Daily Rhythm, Daily Reminders, Bible, authentication and admin behaviour unless explicitly changed below.

PART 1 — THE BIBLE MUST ALWAYS BE AVAILABLE

The Daily Rhythm completion gate must never block My Bible.

Required rule:

- My Bible is always available to every signed-in user.
- Users may open and use the Bible without completing that day’s 10 Minutes with Jesus.
- Bible deep links must also bypass the Daily Rhythm gate.
- Bible links opened from Ask Emmaus, Groups, sermons, Walks, Journeys, notifications or shared URLs must open directly to the correct Bible location.
- Previous Bible-reading position may be restored normally.
- Do not redirect Bible routes into Daily Rhythm.
- Do not weaken the Daily Rhythm gate for other gated content.
- Today’s Steps, Walks, Journeys and other existing gate rules must remain as intended.

Create an explicit central route-access policy instead of scattered exceptions:

Always available:
- My Bible
- Bible book/chapter/verse routes
- Settings
- Account
- Help and permission recovery
- Active meeting re-entry where appropriate

Daily Rhythm gated:
- Only the content already intentionally gated by the product rules

Test Bible access before and after Daily Rhythm completion, on a new day, through direct URLs and from inside a meeting.

PART 2 — PERMISSION PREFLIGHT BEFORE JOINING A MEETING

A participant joined without receiving a working microphone/camera permission prompt. The meeting was delayed while device settings were repaired.

Build a mandatory pre-join device check before entering an audio or video meeting.

The pre-join screen must:

- Detect whether microphone and camera APIs are supported.
- Show the current state separately:
  - Microphone ready
  - Microphone permission needed
  - Microphone blocked in device settings
  - No microphone detected
  - Camera ready
  - Camera permission needed
  - Camera blocked in device settings
  - No camera detected
- Request permissions only from a clear user action such as “Check microphone and camera.”
- Let the participant hear or see a microphone-level indicator.
- Show a local camera preview for video meetings.
- Let the participant choose audio-only even when the meeting supports video.
- Let the participant choose the correct microphone, speaker and camera where the browser supports device selection.
- Explain, in simple device-specific language, how to repair blocked permissions.
- Provide “Open device/browser settings” where the platform allows it.
- Never leave the user waiting without explaining what is wrong.
- Do not claim permission is ready merely because an API exists.
- Recheck immediately when the user returns from device settings.
- Permit joining a voice meeting without camera permission.
- Prevent joining an audio meeting without a usable microphone unless the participant deliberately chooses listen-only.
- Store no permission result as permanently valid; verify the real device state for each meeting.

Add an accessible Help option inside the meeting:

“Microphone or camera not working?”

It must reopen the device check without forcing the participant out of the meeting.

PART 3 — UNIVERSAL MEETING CONTROL DOCK

Microphone, camera and hand controls must remain available throughout the complete meeting.

Create one persistent meeting control dock that is part of the meeting shell, not an individual meeting page.

It must remain visible and functional across:

- Main meeting view
- Chat
- Shared Bible
- Presented PDF
- Images and other media
- Meeting notes
- Participant list
- Any leader presentation screen

Controls:

- Mute/unmute microphone
- Hide/show camera
- Raise/lower hand
- Leave meeting
- Clear current microphone and camera state
- Compact active-speaker/connection indicator where appropriate

Requirements:

- Controls must reflect the actual LiveKit track state, not a separate UI boolean.
- If the track fails to publish, show the real failure and recovery option.
- Do not show the microphone as active when it is not transmitting.
- Raised-hand state must synchronise reliably for leader and participants.
- Controls must remain reachable on narrow mobile screens without covering essential content.
- Respect safe areas and the mobile keyboard.
- Opening chat or shared content must not remount or destroy the meeting controls.
- The participant must never need to navigate back to one special screen to mute.

PART 4 — RELIABLE LEADER-CONTROLLED CHAT

The leader’s “Open Chat for everyone” control was unreliable. With three people, different combinations of participants had chat open or closed. Repeated toggling was required.

Audit the exact state model. Do not repair this with extra client-side toggle events.

Implement authoritative meeting presentation state:

- Store the current shared panel state on the server or meeting state authority:
  - none
  - chat
  - Bible
  - PDF/document
  - image/media
  - other supported presentation
- Include a monotonically increasing version or timestamp.
- When the leader changes the shared panel, publish one authoritative state update.
- Every connected participant applies the newest version idempotently.
- A participant joining late receives the current state immediately.
- A reconnecting participant receives the current state immediately.
- Duplicate or out-of-order events must not reverse the interface.
- Never implement this as “toggle chat.” Send explicit commands:
  - set shared panel to chat/open
  - set shared panel to none/closed
- The leader control must accurately display the current authoritative state.
- If a client fails to apply the state, retry or resynchronise automatically.
- Participants may be allowed to minimise a leader-opened panel locally only if the product explicitly supports that. Clearly separate local minimisation from the leader’s shared state.
- Do not repeatedly force-open a panel after a participant deliberately minimises it unless the leader issues a new presentation action.

Test:

- Three clients already connected
- Late join
- Temporary network interruption
- Reconnect
- Rapid open/close
- Switching chat → Bible → PDF → chat
- Duplicate and delayed events

PART 5 — PRESENTING PDFS AND MEDIA MUST NOT REMOVE USERS

Opening the presented PDF caused participants to leave or disconnect from the meeting.

This is a critical defect.

Required architecture:

- The meeting connection must live in a persistent meeting shell above the changing content views.
- Opening chat, Bible, a PDF, an image or media must not navigate away from or unmount the LiveKit room/provider.
- Do not use normal external-page navigation for presented content.
- Display PDFs inside an in-app viewer, panel, drawer, modal or nested meeting route that preserves the active connection.
- Opening or closing shared content must not call disconnect, leave, cleanup or unpublish tracks.
- Browser Back must return to the meeting view without ending the meeting.
- External files must open safely without replacing the Emmaus PWA unless the user explicitly chooses “Open externally.”
- If “Open externally” is used, clearly warn that the external application may obscure or interrupt the meeting.
- Prefer an embedded viewer with page navigation, zoom and download where permitted.
- The leader may present a document without forcing every participant’s browser to navigate to a new URL.
- Participants must be able to follow the presented document while retaining universal microphone, camera, hand and leave controls.
- Audio must continue uninterrupted while PDFs and images are viewed.
- Camera tracks must remain published if enabled.
- Rejoining must not be required after closing a document.

Audit component lifecycle and report precisely which navigation/unmount behaviour caused the disconnections.

PART 6 — PARTICIPANT CHAT MEDIA UPLOADS

Participants could not upload media in meeting chat.

First inspect the intended permission model.

Required behaviour:

- Ordinary meeting participants may send text and upload the allowed meeting-chat media unless the leader or group policy has deliberately disabled uploads.
- Leader-only presentation privileges must not accidentally block ordinary chat attachments.
- Separate permissions:
  - send text
  - upload chat attachment
  - present content to everyone
  - control shared meeting panel
- Participants may upload attachments to chat.
- Only authorised leaders may force-present content to everyone.
- Show a clear explanation when an upload is unavailable or rejected.
- Never silently fail.
- Display upload progress, success and failure.
- Allow safe file types appropriate to Emmaus:
  - images
  - PDF documents
  - short audio/voice notes where already supported
  - other currently approved media
- Enforce safe server-side MIME validation, file-size limits and access control.
- Do not trust only file extensions.
- Attachments must remain private to the correct group/meeting.
- Members must not access another group’s files by guessing a URL.
- Uploaded content must not contain executable HTML or scripts.
- Avoid loading full large files into browser memory.
- A failed upload must not affect the LiveKit connection.
- Uploading and opening an attachment must not disconnect anyone.

PART 7 — CONNECTION RESILIENCE

Audit why three participants could fall into inconsistent states.

Add clear connection states:

- Connecting
- Connected
- Reconnecting
- Connection degraded
- Disconnected
- Permission blocked

Requirements:

- Temporary network interruption should trigger LiveKit reconnection rather than immediately removing the participant.
- UI panels and meeting state must survive reconnect.
- Restore local microphone/camera intentions after reconnect where permission remains valid.
- Restore the authoritative shared panel after reconnect.
- Do not create duplicate participant sessions.
- Do not mark a participant absent during a brief reconnect.
- Provide a clear Rejoin action only when automatic recovery truly fails.
- Log useful technical diagnostics without recording private meeting content or exposing credentials.

PART 8 — PERFORMANCE AND SCALE

The failed test involved only three participants and was predominantly voice. Do not attribute these problems to scale without evidence.

Run a reliability test with:

- Three independent authenticated clients
- Six simulated or real clients where the environment permits
- Audio-only meeting
- Mixed audio/video meeting
- Chat opened and closed repeatedly
- Bible presentation
- PDF presentation
- Participant image/PDF upload
- Microphone mute/unmute from every panel
- Camera hide/show from every panel
- Hand raise from every panel
- One late join
- One temporary disconnect and reconnect

Measure and report:

- Join success
- Permission-preflight behaviour
- Media-track publication success
- State synchronisation
- Unexpected disconnections
- Duplicate participants
- Chat delivery
- Attachment upload
- PDF presentation
- Reconnection time
- Relevant client/server errors

Do not claim a successful multi-client test by opening three tabs that share one session if that prevents genuine independent participant identities. Use isolated browser contexts or appropriate integration tests.

PART 9 — RELEASE SAFETY

Before changing code:

- Create a checkpoint.
- Map the persistent meeting provider/component hierarchy.
- Identify every route or state transition that can unmount or disconnect the room.
- Identify the authority for chat/presentation state.
- Identify participant-versus-leader upload permissions.
- Identify how meeting permission failures are currently surfaced.

Do not rewrite the complete meeting system unless the audit proves it necessary.

Do not publish automatically.

At completion report:

1. Root cause of every reported problem.
2. Files changed.
3. Database changes.
4. Permission changes.
5. Why the PDF previously disconnected participants.
6. Why chat state diverged.
7. Why participant uploads were blocked.
8. How the meeting provider now survives every content view.
9. Results from three-client and six-client tests.
10. Confirmation that My Bible always bypasses the Daily Rhythm gate.
11. Confirmation that reminder code, scheduler and secrets were not changed.
12. Remaining risks or tests requiring real physical devices.
